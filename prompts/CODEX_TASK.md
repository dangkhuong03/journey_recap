## Non-negotiable scope
The assignment is about the **past four weeks of internship**.

AI20K and the pre-internship engineer background are a concise prologue only.
Do not turn the page into an AI20K autobiography or a career timeline.
Do not create a challenge-by-challenge portfolio or invent a “top challenges” selection.

Recommended page weighting:
- 10–20% prologue
- 60–70% four-week internship journey
- 20–25% evolution/reflection/future direction

## Critical chronology rule
Do **not** create a structure like:
- Week 1 = Apply
- Week 2 = Explore
- Week 3 = Question
- Week 4 = Realign

That framing is inaccurate.

The intern learned different things across individual Day-level challenges, and topics were interleaved throughout the four weeks.

Use:
**Day 1 → Day 27 / a continuous four-week timeline**
as the temporal spine.

Then layer challenge categories and cross-cutting learning patterns onto that timeline.

Possible categories:
- Product
- Business / Strategy
- UX / AI UX
- AI Systems / Agent Architecture
- Infrastructure / Platform
- Evaluation
- Human–AI Collaboration

Do not claim that any category belongs exclusively to one week.

## Objective
Create a polished, responsive, infographic-style visual essay that makes four things obvious:
1. what happened during the internship journey,
2. what was learned,
3. what was achieved,
4. how the intern evolved.

The page should not feel like:
- a dashboard,

- a page that can be scanned quickly but rewards deeper reading.

## Story that must survive implementation
The intern enters the internship with an engineering mindset that AI20K has already expanded with product/value/evidence thinking.

Across the four weeks, different Day-level challenges expose the intern to product, business, UX, AI systems, infrastructure, evaluation, and human–AI collaboration problems.

The learning is scattered across the Days. Looking back, several recurring patterns become clear:
- ask about value and outcome, not only implementation,
- use evidence and research to strengthen decisions,
- direct AI according to personal reasoning rather than follow AI output,
- distinguish completion from understanding/mastery,
- recognize that strong execution is not enough when the intended outcome is misunderstood.

A major later turning point is the mentor conversation that reveals a mismatch between the assumed outcome and the intended outcome.

The resulting principle is:
**ALIGNMENT BEFORE OPTIMIZATION**

The ending synthesizes the evolved mindset and shows Human Evaluation × Development only as a future direction.

## Required page moments
1. **Hero** — communicates “four-week internship + transformation” in <= 5 seconds.
2. **Prologue** — compress engineer background + AI20K + Application → Product into one concise starting-context section.
3. **Day-based journey spine** — visually encode Day 1 → Day 27 / four weeks without assigning one fixed topic to each week.
4. **Challenge-category layer** — show breadth across Product, Business, UX, AI Systems, Infrastructure, Evaluation, and Human–AI Collaboration without turning the page into a challenge portfolio.
5. **What happened** — show the internship as a repeated learning environment: broad challenge → framing → scope → research/experience → AI support → audit → feedback → reflection.
6. **What I learned** — prioritize cross-cutting lessons: Value before technology; Evidence before assumption; Human judgment over AI output; Alignment before optimization.
7. **Human-directed AI workflow** — MY INTENT → MY APPROACH → AI SUPPORT → RESEARCH/CRITIQUE → AUDIT → ADJUST → MY DECISION.
8. **What I achieved** — visibly show achievement through Breadth of Exposure / Applied Practice / Behavior Change. Do not invent metrics.
9. **Major turning point** — outcome misalignment with mentor; strong visual treatment of “Alignment before optimization.” Do not label this as a fixed Week 4 phase.
10. **Evolution** — strong Before vs Now comparison based on changed questions, decisions, workflow, and behavior.
11. **Learning loop** — Experience → Learn → Reflect → Select → Apply → Feedback → Update → Repeat.
12. **Final direction** — Human Evaluation × Development as an emerging direction, not a claimed finished title.
