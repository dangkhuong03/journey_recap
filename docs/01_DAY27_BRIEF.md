# Day 27 — Journey Recap Brief

## Original challenge
> Design an impressive infographic-style landing page that captures and reflects your journey over the past 4 weeks. The page should tell a clear visual story of what happened, what was learned, what was achieved, and how the intern evolved throughout the journey.

## What the challenge actually asks for
The deliverable is an information-storytelling landing page about the **past four weeks of internship**.

It is not primarily:
- an AI20K autobiography
- a generic portfolio
- a challenge gallery
- a chronological activity log
- a visual-effects demo

Earlier AI20K experience can be used as a concise prologue to establish the starting mindset, but the four internship weeks must remain the visual and narrative center of gravity.

## Four mandatory questions
The page must answer clearly:
1. **What happened during the four weeks?**
2. **What was learned?**
3. **What was achieved?**
4. **How did the intern evolve?**

## Interpretation of “what was achieved”
Achievement does not require fake statistics or inflated claims.
It may include:
- concepts understood and applied more concretely
- a more deliberate problem-solving workflow
- stronger use of evidence and research in decisions
- clearer human judgment over AI-generated output
- improved understanding of alignment, evaluation, and user/customer context
- an emerging direction connecting development and human evaluation

Use factual artifacts or metrics only if they exist in the source. Never invent them.

## North star
Do not design a page that tries to show everything the intern did.
Design a page that makes a reviewer understand:
- what the four-week experience contained,
- what changed because of it,
- and how that change became visible in the intern's decisions and way of working.

## Recommended narrative weighting
- 10–20%: Before internship / AI20K context
- 60–70%: Four-week internship journey
- 20–25%: Evolution + reflection + next direction

## Audience assumption
Primary audience:
- mentors
- reviewers
- AI/product/design practitioners
- people evaluating the intern's growth

The reviewer should not need to read every paragraph. The page must work both as:
- a 30–60 second scan
- a deeper 5–10 minute read
