# Acceptance Criteria / Submit Audit — Revised

## A. Exact challenge fit
- [ ] Clearly reads as a recap of the **past four internship weeks**.
- [ ] At least 60% of the page's narrative/visual weight is about the internship four-week experience, learning, feedback, achievements, and evolution.
- [ ] Pre-internship / AI20K context is clearly a prologue and does not dominate the page.
- [ ] Clearly communicates **what happened**.
- [ ] Clearly communicates **what was learned**.
- [ ] Clearly communicates **what was achieved**.
- [ ] Clearly communicates **how the intern evolved**.

## B. What happened
- [ ] The page has a visible Day 1 → Day 27 / four-week temporal spine.
- [ ] It communicates the internship as a learning environment involving challenges, research, technical exploration, tests, feedback, and mentor interaction.
- [ ] It does not fabricate a precise weekly diary when exact chronology is not known.
- [ ] It does not assign one fixed learning theme to Week 1, Week 2, Week 3, or Week 4.
- [ ] Cross-cutting lessons are presented as patterns extracted from the whole journey, not as week-specific curriculum.
- [ ] It does not require a challenge-by-challenge portfolio section.

## C. What was learned
- [ ] Core lessons are prioritized instead of presenting every topic with equal weight.
- [ ] Value before technology is visible.
- [ ] Evidence before assumption is visible.
- [ ] Human judgment over AI output is visible.
- [ ] Alignment before optimization is visible.
- [ ] Technical topics such as agent/context/memory/RAG/tools/HITL/evaluation are supporting knowledge, not the whole story.

## D. What was achieved
- [ ] There is an identifiable achievement section or visual layer.
- [ ] Achievements are framed honestly without fake numbers.
- [ ] Achievement includes increased understanding where supported by the source.
- [ ] Achievement includes applied practice / changed workflow where supported by the source.
- [ ] Achievement includes behavioral change in how problems, evidence, AI support, and alignment are handled.
- [ ] The page does not equate “completed” with “mastered.”

## E. Evolution
- [ ] There is one central transformation story, not disconnected themes.
- [ ] Starting state is visible.
- [ ] Ending state is visibly different from starting state.
- [ ] Before/Now is visually encoded rather than explained only through prose.
- [ ] The engineer mindset is shown as expanded, not rejected.
- [ ] Evolution is expressed through changed questions, decisions, workflow, and behavior — not only a skill list.

## F. Turning point
- [ ] The mentor outcome-alignment conversation is treated as the major turning point.
- [ ] It clearly communicates that good execution can still miss the mark when the intended outcome is misunderstood.
- [ ] “Alignment before optimization” is visible as a core lesson.
- [ ] The framing is reflective and non-blaming.

## G. Evidence and honesty
- [ ] No fake metrics.
- [ ] No fabricated quotes.
- [ ] No fabricated week-by-week facts or artificial “one topic per week” narrative.
- [ ] No unsupported claims of mastery.
- [ ] Growth claims are tied to changed reasoning, workflow, decisions, or experiences from the source.
- [ ] If information is missing, the implementation uses neutral wording or TODOs rather than invention.

## H. Human + AI story
- [ ] The intern is clearly the person directing the solution approach.
- [ ] AI is shown as a support tool for research, critique, perspective expansion, and checking.
- [ ] Human judgment remains responsible for final decisions.
- [ ] The page does not imply that AI did the challenges autonomously.

## I. Information design
- [ ] Four-week progression is visually encoded through a truthful Day-based or continuous timeline.
- [ ] Evolution is visually encoded.
- [ ] Achievement is visually encoded.
- [ ] At least one process/system diagram is used.
- [ ] The learning loop is visualized clearly.
- [ ] Visuals reveal relationships, change, causality, or evidence — not decoration only.
- [ ] Charts are only used when real quantitative data exists.

## J. Visual hierarchy
- [ ] A 5-second hero test communicates “four-week internship journey + transformation.”
- [ ] A 30–60 second scan reveals the four mandatory brief answers.
- [ ] Primary, secondary, tertiary text levels are obvious.
- [ ] Each section has a clear focal point.
- [ ] Body copy does not dominate the page.
- [ ] Technical terms do not overpower the human growth story.

## K. Motion
- [ ] Motion communicates progression, causality, reveal, or transformation.
- [ ] Reduced-motion preference is respected.
- [ ] Animation does not block reading or navigation.
- [ ] The page still works when motion is disabled.

## L. Product craft
- [ ] Responsive desktop/tablet/mobile.
- [ ] Semantic structure and accessible contrast.
- [ ] Keyboard navigation works where interaction exists.
- [ ] No obvious overflow/layout failures.
- [ ] Loading/performance remains reasonable.
- [ ] No console errors in final build.

## M. Final reviewer tests
- [ ] After 5 seconds: reviewer knows this is a four-week internship Journey Recap.
- [ ] After 30 seconds: reviewer understands what happened and at least two major lessons.
- [ ] After 60 seconds: reviewer understands what the intern achieved and the major mentor-feedback turning point.
- [ ] After full read: reviewer can describe how the intern's thinking and working process evolved.
- [ ] Human Evaluation × Development appears as a logical next direction, not as the main story or an overclaimed title.
